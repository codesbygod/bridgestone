Overview

Harbourline is a 40 storey office tower on a reclaimed waterfront plot. The client wanted open floor plates and a slim silhouette, so we moved the lateral system to the perimeter as a steel diagrid and kept the core to lifts and stairs. Every node was modelled parametrically, and the frame was checked under full wind tunnel loading before a single connection was drawn.

The challenge

The plot is reclaimed land on an exposed waterfront, and the client wanted open, column-free floors inside a slim silhouette. A conventional concrete core and moment frame would have eaten into the lettable area and made the tower too heavy for the ground beneath it.

Our approach

We moved the lateral system to the perimeter as a steel diagrid, so the facade carries both gravity and wind load and the core is left to lifts, stairs and risers. The tower was tested in a boundary-layer wind tunnel at concept stage, and all 1,100 diagrid nodes were generated parametrically from the same model the fabricator used to cut steel.

What we delivered

  • Concept and scheme design of the diagrid frame
  • Wind tunnel testing and occupant comfort analysis
  • Parametric node design and fabrication models
  • Piled raft foundation on reclaimed ground
  • Site supervision through erection and handover

The outcome

The frame used about a fifth less steel than the braced-core alternative, every floor is free of internal columns, and the tower topped out three weeks ahead of programme. Sway at the top floor stays inside comfort limits in a one-in-ten-year storm.
